**What is the most interesting topic covered in your domain this quarter?**

The topic I find most interesting is how genetic variation influences gene expression through expression quantitative trait loci (eQTLs). I'm interested in connecting differences in DNA sequences to changes in gene activity because this can help us investigate how genetic variants may contribute to complex diseases. This adds a biological perspective to the statistical associations we study.

**Describe a potential investigation you would like to pursue for your Quarter 2 Project.**

I would like to investigate whether incorporating gene-expression evidence into polygenic risk scores improves their predictive performance across populations. I would focus on one disease and compare our Quarter 1 pruning-and-thresholding approach with an alternative that prioritizes variants supported by cis-eQTL evidence. Using an independent dataset with genotypes and disease outcomes, I would evaluate whether this approach improves prediction consistently across ancestry groups. If suitable outcome data are unavailable, I would use simulated traits to test when expression-informed scores help or hurt performance. This would extend our current work by testing a specific hypothesis about how functional information could improve genetic prediction.

**What is a potential change you'd make to the approach taken in your current Quarter 1 Project?**

I would spread out my reading of the papers provided by our mentor so I have more time to digest and understand the concepts. Between papers, I would review unfamiliar terms, take notes, and connect the methods to our project. This would help me build a stronger understanding before applying the techniques in our analysis.

**What other techniques would you be interested in using in your project?**

I would be interested in statistical fine-mapping and colocalization. Fine-mapping could help narrow down which variants are most likely to explain an association, especially when nearby variants are correlated. Colocalization could help assess whether a gene-expression association and a disease association share the same underlying causal variant. These techniques would help me investigate possible biological mechanisms behind the associations we identify, while recognizing that they would not prove causation.
